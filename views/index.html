Guide

How to save Instagram photos, videos and Reels — the full guide

Instagram gives you no built-in way to save a post you don't own, which is exactly why tools like InstaGrab exist. Here's what actually works, why it works, and what to keep in mind before you download anything.

Why people download Instagram content

Most of the time it's simple: a friend posted a video from a trip and you want it on your own phone, a business wants to reuse its own Reel in an ad, or you found a carousel of reference photos for a project. Instagram is built for scrolling and reacting in the app, not for taking things with you — downloading fills that gap.

Step-by-step: downloading any post

The process is the same regardless of whether the link points to a photo, a video, or a Reel:

  1. Open the post inside the Instagram app or on instagram.com.
  2. Tap the three dots above the post (or the paper-plane "Share" icon) and choose Copy Link.
  3. Paste that link into the box at the top of this page.
  4. InstaGrab reads the link, works out whether it's a photo, carousel, video, or Reel, and shows a preview with a download button.

Carousels and photo albums

A carousel post is really several photos or videos bundled behind one link. When InstaGrab detects one, it lays out every slide individually with its own save link, plus a single Download All button so you don't have to save each slide one at a time. Video slides inside a carousel are marked so you can tell them apart from photos before downloading.

Reels and videos in full quality

Reels and feed videos are fetched from the same source Instagram's own web player uses, so the file you get is the same resolution and bitrate as the original upload — not a re-encoded or watermarked copy. There's nothing extra to configure; paste the link and the correct format is detected automatically.

If a link ever fails with a message about logging in, it usually means the account or post has extra privacy restrictions Instagram applies at random to some content — this is rare for public accounts and isn't something InstaGrab can bypass by design.

Is it legal and safe to download Instagram content?

Saving a public post to your own device for personal use — rewatching it, keeping a copy of something you were tagged in, archiving your own content — is generally not a problem. What matters is what you do with it afterward: reposting someone else's photo or video as your own, or using it commercially without permission, can run into copyright issues regardless of which tool you used to save it. When in doubt, ask the original creator or credit them clearly.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to log in to my Instagram account?

No. Public photos, carousels, videos, and Reels can be downloaded without logging in. A login is only needed for the rare private or restricted post.

Will the downloaded file have a watermark?

No. InstaGrab fetches the original media file Instagram itself serves, so there's no added watermark or quality loss.

Can I download a whole carousel or album at once?

Yes. Paste the link to a multi-photo or multi-video carousel post and InstaGrab will show every slide with a Download All option.

Is downloading Instagram content legal?

Saving a public post for personal use is generally fine, but the content still belongs to its original creator. Don't re-upload, resell, or claim someone else's photo or video as your own.
